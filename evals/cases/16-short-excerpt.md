The rain had not stopped for three days when Elena found the violin.

It lay in a case at the back of the pawnshop, beneath a stack of yellowed sheet music and a broken metronome. The shop smelled of damp wool and old varnish, and somewhere beneath it, the sharp, resinous scent of rosin. Elena's breath caught in her throat. She had not touched a violin in six years, not since the night of the accident, not since her hands had stopped belonging to her.

She lifted the lid. The instrument inside was old, its varnish worn pale at the shoulder where some other player's chin had rested for decades. A hairline crack ran along the lower bout, carefully repaired. Her fingers hovered above the strings, trembling.

"She's been waiting a long time," said the shopkeeper.

Elena turned. He was a small man with silver spectacles and a cardigan buttoned wrong, and he watched her with an expression she could not read.

"Instruments don't wait," she said. "They're just wood and string."

"Are they?" He came around the counter slowly. "Then why do you think it hurts to look at one?"

Her chest tightened. She set the lid down gently, as if it might break. "I don't play anymore."

"I didn't ask if you played," the old man said. "I asked why it hurts."

Outside, the rain drummed against the window, and a bus hissed past, throwing gray water across the glass. Elena pressed her palm against the cool wood of the case. She could feel her pulse in her fingertips, quick and uneven, the way it used to feel in the wings before a performance.

"Because I was good," she said finally. "Because it was the one thing I was sure of. And then it was gone, and I had to become someone else."

The shopkeeper nodded, as if she had said something he had been expecting for a long time. He reached beneath the counter and drew out a small block of rosin, wrapped in faded cloth, and set it beside the case.

"Sometimes," he said, "the person we become is only the bridge back to the person we were."

Elena stared at the rosin. Her hands were shaking now, openly, and she did not try to hide it.
