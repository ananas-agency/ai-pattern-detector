# Best Project Tools for Small Agencies in 2026

Choosing a project tool shapes how your agency plans work, tracks hours, and keeps clients informed every week. We compared three tools that small agencies ask us about most often during onboarding calls this year. Each one suits a different team size, budget, and way of handling client feedback on live projects.

## At a glance

Tallyfold

Best for: teams of 5 to 15

From $9 per user per month

Brightdesk

Best for: client-heavy studios

From $14 per user per month

Quorra

Best for: freelancers and pairs

Free plan available

## Tallyfold

Tallyfold keeps every task, timer, and invoice in one place, so project managers rarely switch between separate apps. Its weekly planning board shows who is overbooked before the week starts, which helps leads rebalance work early. The reporting module is the strongest of the three, although the mobile app still lacks offline editing for tasks.

What we liked

Built-in time tracking

Clear capacity view

What to watch

No offline mode on mobile

## Brightdesk

Brightdesk gives clients their own portal, where they approve drafts, leave comments, and download final files without email. Agencies that handle many small client requests will find its intake forms save hours of manual sorting. Its pricing climbs quickly once you add guest seats, so larger client lists can push the monthly bill up.

What we liked

Client approval portal

Custom intake forms

What to watch

Guest seats cost extra

## Quorra

Quorra is the lightest of the three tools, with a simple board, a shared calendar, and basic file storage. Freelancers and two-person studios can run it on the free plan for months before they need paid features. Teams that bill by the hour will miss proper time tracking, which Quorra only offers through a separate add-on.

Source: vendor pricing pages, checked October 2026

## Which tool should you pick?

Pick Tallyfold if your main problem is capacity planning, and Brightdesk if client approvals slow every project down. Quorra makes sense when the budget is tight and the team is small enough to coordinate in one chat. Whichever tool you choose, run a two-week trial with one real project before moving the whole agency across.
