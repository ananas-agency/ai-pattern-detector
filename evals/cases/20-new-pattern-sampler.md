Every product team I've worked with eventually hits the same wall: the roadmap gets longer, and the releases get slower.

I'm not saying planning doesn't matter. A team without any plan drifts. But somewhere along the way, planning turned into a substitute for shipping, and most teams didn't notice.

This doesn't mean you should stop planning. It means you should plan in smaller pieces and put something in front of users every two weeks, even when it feels unfinished.

Momentum is the currency of small teams. When people see their work go live, they make faster decisions and stop polishing features nobody has asked for yet.

Is it worth the risk of shipping something rough? Honestly? Most of the time, yes. The bugs you find in week two cost far less than the assumptions you carry for six months.

Here's how we changed our process last quarter:

🚀 We cut every roadmap item down to something one engineer can ship in a sprint.
💡 We replaced the monthly planning meeting with a fifteen-minute Monday check-in.
✅ We moved the release checklist into the pull request template, so nothing waits for a sign-off meeting.

Our release count went from four last spring to eleven this quarter, and the support queue didn't grow.
