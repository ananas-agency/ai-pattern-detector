The Riverside Community Garden — first planted by a handful of neighbors in 2015 — has grown into one of the most vibrant green spaces in the district. Every weekend, volunteers gather to tend the raised beds, swap seeds, and share advice on everything from soil health to pest control.

The heart of the garden is its tool shed — a converted shipping container donated by a local hardware store — where members sign out everything they need. A rotating schedule ensures that watering, weeding, and compost turning are handled consistently throughout the growing season.

Education plays a crucial role as well. Monthly workshops — often led by retired teachers and master gardeners — cover topics such as companion planting, seed saving, and composting basics. These sessions have proven especially popular with families looking for hands-on weekend activities.

The garden also supports the wider community. Surplus produce — tomatoes, squash, and leafy greens in particular — is delivered each week to the food pantry on Elm Street, providing fresh options for residents who might otherwise go without.

Looking ahead, the organizers hope to add a pollinator meadow and a small greenhouse. Fundraising efforts — including a spring plant sale and a community dinner in the fall — are already underway, and the garden's leadership remains committed to fostering connection for years to come.
